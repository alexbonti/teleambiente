# Worked example: trend → pitch → approved article

One complete pass, using a real, evergreen trend. Note the order: the trend is real and
linked, the fiction is invented and named, and the label bookends the piece.

## The real trend

Airlines now earn tens of billions a year in ancillary fees — seat selection, earlier
boarding, bags — and the category keeps growing. The straight-faced logic: every part of
the airport experience can be priced separately. One turn further: the airline prices the
act of being acknowledged as a person.

Real detail to keep: the fee category is called "ancillary revenue"; charges attach to
seat selection and boarding order; the numbers are published in airline earnings.

## The article

> **SATIRE — NOT REAL NEWS.** This is a comedy piece. Meridian Air, its "Priority
> Existence" programme and everyone quoted here are invented. It is based on a real
> trend: airlines' add-on fees keep hitting records (link: a real airline

# Meridian Air Introduces $49 'Priority Existence' Tier, Includes Being Acknowledged By Gate Agent

**Programme offers travellers the option to be addressed by name, frowned at with concern**

SEATAC — Meridian Air will begin selling a "Priority Existence" upgrade next month that
lets passengers be acknowledged by name at the gate, the carrier said Tuesday, becoming
the first major airline to charge separately for the basic experience of being perceived.

Under the programme, a $49 fee guarantees that a gate agent will look up from the scanner
and say the passenger's name out loud. An $89 "Warm Welcome" tier adds a sympathetic
frown and the phrase "rough one today, huh?" A $129 "Full Person" package includes one
question about the traveller's destination, asked in a tone suggesting genuine curiosity.

"People told us they wanted to feel seen," said Dale Rooke, Meridian's vice president of
revenue experience, who described the rollout as "the most human thing we have ever put
behind a paywall." He added: "For years we gave away eye contact for free. That is not
sustainable."

Rooke said the programme followed a record quarter in ancillary revenue and that internal
testing found passengers would pay "surprisingly" for a second glance. An airport pilot
will also test a $19 "Ambient Acknowledgment" option, in which a nearby employee nods in
the traveller's general direction without establishing which traveller they meant.

The Passenger Council, an advocacy group, criticised the tier structure, noting that basic
courtesy now costs $49 while the standard fare still includes "being physically present in
the building."

"You get on the plane either way," said spokesperson Marla Feen. "What is being sold here
is the memory of a person looking at you before you sit in 34B for four hours."

Meridian reported a waitlist of 12,000 for the Warm Welcome tier and said the $6 option to
have an agent say "no worries" instead of "next" is already sold out.

The company plans to expand Priority Existence to twelve more airports by spring.

**What's real here:** airlines now earn tens of billions a year from add-on fees, and
charging for seat selection and boarding order is standard. Everything about Meridian Air,
its tiers, its spokespeople and its numbers is invented for comedy.

## Why this works

- **Anchors in the real world:** the fee category, the pricing logic and the earnings
  language are real; only the extension is fake.
- **Escalation, not repetition:** fee → subscription tiers → waitlist → sold-out $6 tier.
  Each paragraph adds a worse, more specific product.
- **Deadpan voices:** Rooke's justification is coherent and horrifying; Feen states the
  obvious flatly. Nobody in the article thinks anything is funny.
- **Kicker does no work but the last twist:** the sold-out six-dollar "no worries" line
  costs nothing to read and lands the whole premise.
- **Labeled twice:** banner claims nothing is real, footer states exactly what is.
