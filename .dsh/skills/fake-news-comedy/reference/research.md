# Trend research

Only after Checkpoint 1. The output of this step is a short research digest (10 bullets
max) plus the pitch card. Do not show the digest as a wall of text — it exists so the
pitch has real detail behind it.

## What counts as a trend

- Something real and checkable that happened or spiked in roughly the last two weeks.
- Prefer things the audience has already seen: a viral clip, a price move, a policy, a
  study, a product launch, a discourse war, a phrase everyone suddenly uses.
- "Trendy" is not "important". A small absurd true detail beats a serious macro story,
  because the article needs one concrete thing to hold up.
- If the topic has no fresh angle, say so plainly and offer a timeless angle instead of
  faking currency. Never invent a "this week" claim.

## Search recipe

Two to four `web_search` calls, then `web_fetch` the two best results. Adapt the topic
into the queries:

1. `<topic> news this week` — what happened.
2. `<topic> viral OR trending OR backlash` — what people are arguing about.
3. `site:reddit.com <topic>` — the vocabulary people actually use; gold for phrasing.
4. `<topic> study OR report <current year>` — a "new research shows" anchor.

Capture, for each usable item: publication, date, link, and one quotable concrete detail
(a number, a policy name, a price, a place, a job title).

## Trend radar ("you pick")

When the user delegates the topic:

1. Search `trending topics today`, plus one or two niche verticals that suit the user.
2. Pick three candidates that are (a) current, (b) checkable, (c) absurd in an
   institutional way, not a tragedy.
3. Offer them in three lines, each as `topic — the absurd angle in one line`.
4. Wait for a choice. Do not pick for the user unless they say "any" — then pick the
   first and move to Checkpoint 2 with the pitch card.

## Fact/fiction ledger

Write this down before the pitch card, and keep it through the draft:

- **Real (keep):** two to four facts with source and date — an institution's name, a
  number, a real phrase, a real place, a real job title.
- **Fake (invent):** masthead, byline, the company or agency at the centre, its
  spokespeople, the study, the quotes, the absurd policy, its tiers and numbers.
- **Never invent:** quotes attributed to real named people; real statistics; real
  studies; anything actionable about health, safety, money, elections or the law.

The article's credibility comes from the real half. The joke comes from the invented
half. Keep them visibly separate and the satire stays clean.

## The comedy seam

The best pitch is not "the trend is funny". It is "the trend, extended one turn past
where reality stopped, is absurd".

Ask: what is the straight-faced, official logic of this trend? Then follow that logic to
its next step. The gap between reality and that step is the article.

- Real: airlines sold billions in seat-selection and boarding fees. Next step: the
  airline sells being acknowledged by name at the gate.
- Real: companies track badge swipes for hybrid work. Next step: an office installs a
  lobby leaderboard of "presence scores".
- Real: everyone uses an AI assistant to draft messages. Next step: a company requires
  employees to have their apologies drafted by the AI first, for legal reasons.

Two tests for a candidate premise:

1. **One line** — can you say the joke in one sentence with no "and then"?
2. **Institutional, not personal** — does the absurdity belong to a system, a policy, a
   product or a price, rather than to a body or an identity? If it is personal, reroll.

## Handing to the pitch

The pitch card needs exactly: the trend sentence with link, the premise, why now, the
real facts kept, the fiction invented, format/language/length, and the risk check. If a
field is missing, you have not finished researching.
