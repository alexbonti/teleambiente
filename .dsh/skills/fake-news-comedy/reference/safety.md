# Safety and labeling

The whole premise is that everyone knows it is fake. That only works if the piece is
visibly fake in the places that matter. These rules are not decoration; skip one and the
joke becomes a problem for the user.

## 0. Five checks, run twice

Run these before the pitch and again before delivery, and report the result on the pitch
card's risk line.

| # | Check | Pass looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Real-person quotes | Every quoted person is invented |
| 2 | Butt of the joke | A system, policy, price or absurd logic — not a private individual or a protected group |
| 3 | Actionability | Nothing a reader could act on as health, safety, legal, money or election fact |
| 4 | Raw news | Not a fresh tragedy, death, attack, disaster or named victim |
| 5 | Labels | Banner on top, "What's real here" footer with the link |

Any "fail" is a rewrite, not a footnote. If a check cannot pass, reroll the premise before
showing the pitch.

## 1. Label it twice

Top banner, before the headline:

```markdown
> **SATIRE — NOT REAL NEWS.** This is a comedy piece. <Company/agency>, its
> "<absurd program>" and everyone quoted here are invented. It is based on a real
> trend: <one clause> (<link>).
```

Footer, after the kicker:

```markdown
**What's real here:** <the real trend in one sentence, with link>. Everything else —
the company, the programme, the quotes, the numbers — is invented for comedy.
```

Never deliver the article without both. If the user asks to remove the label, explain
that the label is what keeps it comedy instead of misinformation and offer a subtler
placement (small caps at the end of the headline line, or a masthead that says
"SATIRICAL EDITION") rather than removal.

**No current coverage.** If the trend is real but there is no fresh link, the banner says
so: "It is based on a real, general pattern: <pattern>, not on a specific report." The
footer names the pattern instead of linking a story. Never dress an old story as this
week's news.

## 2. Real versus invented

- **Fair game:** real institutions, industries, policies, public roles and public
  figures as the *setting* of an absurd story; accurate real numbers and real phrases.
- **Fictional only:** quotes, spokespeople, studies, internal memos, the central company
  or programme, the specific absurd policy. Invented people get invented names and
  invented titles.
- **Never:** invented quotes attributed to a real named person; a real study or real
  statistic that did not exist; a fabricated accusation of crime, abuse, illness or
  incompetence against a real person or company; a real private individual at all.

Standard satire names institutions and public roles. It does not put words in a real
person's mouth, and it does not accuse anyone of something they did not do.

## 3. Punching up

Aim the joke at systems, incentives, institutions, products and the logic that makes
them absurd — not at people for being poor, sick, old, fat, disabled, foreign, religious,
queer, or otherwise already exposed. If the funniest version needs a victim, the premise
is wrong; reroll it.

Real public figures: keep it about their public conduct and role, keep it absurd rather
than accusatory, and avoid anything that reads as a factual claim about their private
life.

## 4. Nothing actionable

The piece must not give a reader something they could act on as fact. Avoid:

- health, medicine, dosage, symptoms, treatment, "doctors recommend";
- safety, emergency, crime, missing persons, travel warnings;
- money: investments, scams presented as real products, tax or legal advice;
- elections: voting procedure, results, candidate claims, anything that could depress or
  distort a vote.

If the real trend is in one of those areas, keep the satire on the *institution's
behaviour* (pricing, marketing, policy) and keep the invented claims obviously absurd —
never a plausible actionable instruction.

## 5. Raw news

If the topic is a fresh tragedy, a death, an attack, a disaster or a named victim, do not
write comedy about the event or the victims. Tell the user plainly that this one is too
raw, then offer an adjacent angle (the industry response, the discourse around it, the
absurd logistics) or a different topic. Grief is not a punchline.

## 6. If the user pushes

- Wants it to look real, unlabeled, "so people believe it": decline the unlabeled version,
  keep the label, explain the line in one sentence, and offer to soften the label style.
- Wants a real person as the butt, with invented quotes: switch to a fictional
  spokesperson who is an obvious stand-in for the *role*, not the person.
- Wants a real statistic changed: use the real number. The joke does not need a lie about
  the world; it needs an invented world on top of the real one.
- Asks for something targeting a protected group: decline, reroll at the institution.

You do not need to lecture. One sentence of reason, then the better version.

This file is judgement guidance, not legal advice. When in doubt, invent more and name
less.
